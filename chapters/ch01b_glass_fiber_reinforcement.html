Glass Fiber and Reinforcement: Types, Specifications, Performance

The reinforcement defines the structural ceiling

While the resin provides the matrix and processing characteristics, the reinforcement (primarily glass fiber) sets the upper bound on mechanical performance — tensile and flexural strength, impact resistance, fatigue life, and creep resistance. Fiber type, content, length, sizing, and orientation distribution are the critical variables.

1B.1 Why Glass Fiber Dominates SMC

Glass fiber advantages

  • Excellent strength-to-cost ratio (10–20× cheaper than carbon fiber)
  • Good tensile strength (3,400–4,900 MPa depending on glass type)
  • Chemical inertness in most environments
  • Well-developed sizing chemistry for UPR/VE/epoxy systems
  • Non-conductive (electrical insulation applications)
  • Established supply chain with tight quality control

1B.3 Fiber Forms for SMC

Roving to chopped strand

SMC uses chopped glass strands cut from continuous rovings directly on the SMC manufacturing line. Key specifications:

Parameter Typical Range Engineering Significance
Roving tex (g/km) 2,400 – 4,800 Higher tex = fewer strand ends per area, better wet-out but coarser distribution
Chop length (mm) 12.5, 25, 50 Longer fibers → higher strength but harder to flow; 25mm is standard
Filament diameter (µm) 13 – 24 Finer filaments improve surface quality; coarser improve throughput
Strand integrity (binder %) 0.3–1.0% LOI Maintains bundle during handling; affects dispersion in resin paste
Moisture content <0.1% Excess moisture → porosity/blistering in molded parts

Direct vs assembled rovings

Direct rovings

  • Single forming position; filaments wound directly into roving package
  • More uniform tex; better chopability; less fuzz/broken filaments
  • Preferred for high-speed SMC lines

Assembled rovings

  • Multiple strands combined; wider tex range possible
  • Can optimize for specific chopping/dispersion behavior
  • More common in traditional/lower-speed lines

1B.4 Sizing Chemistry

Sizing is a thin coating applied during fiber manufacturing (0.3–1.5% by weight) that serves multiple functions:

  • Coupling agent (silane): creates a chemical bridge between the glass surface (SiOH groups) and the resin matrix
  • Film former: binds filaments into a strand; provides handling integrity
  • Lubricant: reduces filament-to-filament abrasion during processing
  • Antistatic agent: prevents charge buildup on glass packages
Silane Type Compatible Resin Key Feature
Methacryloxy silane (A-174) UPR, VE C=C co-reacts with styrene crosslinking; most common for SMC
Amino silane (A-1100) Epoxy, phenolic Amine reacts with epoxide ring; excellent adhesion for epoxy SMC
Vinyl silane (A-151) UPR Cost-effective; moderate bond strength; general-purpose UPR grades
Epoxy silane (A-187) Epoxy, VE Epoxide group reacts with amine/acid hardener; good water resistance
Wrong sizing = catastrophic adhesion failure

Using a UPR-optimized sizing with an epoxy resin (or vice versa) can reduce composite interlaminar shear strength by 30–60%. Always verify sizing compatibility with the resin system before qualifying a new glass supply.

1B.6 Carbon Fiber SMC

Carbon fiber SMC (CF-SMC) is increasingly used in automotive for lightweighting where the higher stiffness-to-weight ratio justifies the cost premium. Key points:

  • Modulus advantage: CF-SMC at 50% fiber → flexural modulus 35–50 GPa vs 16–22 GPa for glass SMC
  • Weight savings: 15–25% lighter than equivalent-performance glass SMC panels
  • EMI shielding: carbon fiber is conductive → provides inherent electromagnetic shielding (useful for EV electronics)
  • Cost: 4–8× glass SMC; tow-spreading and recycled-carbon approaches are closing the gap
  • Processing: lower coverage ratios needed (carbon flows differently); sizing must be reformulated

Hybrid glass/carbon SMC

Some formulations blend glass and carbon fibers (e.g., 20% carbon + 20% glass) to achieve intermediate stiffness at reduced cost. The carbon provides modulus, the glass provides impact toughness and cost balance. This approach is used in automotive semi-structural parts like seat frames and tailgate inners.

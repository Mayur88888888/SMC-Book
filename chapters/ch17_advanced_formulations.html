Advanced SMC formulations: 15-ingredient systems and specialty grades

Formulation is leverage

A well-designed SMC formulation balances processability, mechanical performance, surface quality, cost, and specialty requirements (FST, chemical resistance, low styrene). Each ingredient plays a non-trivial role; changing one often requires compensating adjustments elsewhere.

17.1 The 15-ingredient framework

Modern SMC formulations routinely contain 10–15 functionally distinct ingredients. The table below classifies them by function, then provides standard ranges and selection logic.

# Ingredient Function Typical Range (phr) Key Selection Criteria
1 Unsaturated polyester / vinyl ester / epoxy Matrix resin 100 (base) Tg, HDT, shrinkage, chemical resistance, cost
2 Styrene (or alternative monomer) Reactive diluent / viscosity reducer 25–45 Viscosity target, emissions regulation, cure speed
3 Initiator(s) Free-radical catalyst 0.5–3.0 Onset temperature, gel time, dual-initiator strategy for thick parts
4 Inhibitor Shelf-life stabilizer 0.005–0.05 Storage temperature, maturation rate, demold time
5 Calcium carbonate (CaCO₃) Filler / cost reduction 50–150 Shrinkage control, viscosity, surface texture, cost
6 Thickener (MgO / ZnO) Rheology modifier 1–4 Maturation rate, initial viscosity, green strength
7 Internal mold release (IMR) Demolding aid 1–4 Release force, surface finish, paintability
8 Low-profile additive (LPA) Shrinkage compensation 5–15 Surface quality (Class A), warpage control
9 Pigment / colorant Appearance 0.5–5 Color stability, heat resistance, effect on cure
10 Glass fiber reinforcement Structural reinforcement 20–60 wt% Strength, modulus, impact, fiber length, sizing
11 Thermoplastic modifier Toughness / impact 2–10 Impact resistance, crack propagation, shrinkage offset
12 Flame retardant (FR) Fire performance 10–50 LOI, UL 94, smoke generation, halogen-free requirements
13 Surfactant / wetting agent Wet-out / dispersion 0.1–1.5 Fiber wetting, filler dispersion, air release
14 UV stabilizer Weatherability 0.2–2.0 Outdoor exposure, paint retention, color stability
15 Coupling agent / silane Fiber-matrix adhesion 0.1–0.5 Interfacial strength, moisture resistance, sizing compatibility

17.2 Standard formulation families (by application)

17.2.1 General-purpose structural SMC

Target: balanced mechanical properties at low cost. Used for brackets, non-cosmetic covers, and general industrial parts.

  • Resin: Orthophthalic UPR (100 phr)
  • Styrene: 35 phr
  • Initiator: TBPB (1.5 phr)
  • Inhibitor: HQ (150 ppm)
  • Filler: CaCO₃ (80 phr)
  • Thickener: MgO (2.5 phr)
  • IMR: Zn stearate (2 phr)
  • Fiber: 25% chopped E-glass (25 mm)
  • Typical properties: flexural strength 90–120 MPa, modulus 9–12 GPa

17.2.2 Class A cosmetic SMC

Target: Class A surface, low shrinkage, high gloss, good paintability. Used for automotive body panels, hoods, fenders.

  • Resin: Isophthalic UPR (100 phr)
  • Styrene: 30 phr
  • Initiator: Dual (TBPEH + TBPB, total 2.0 phr)
  • Inhibitor: BHT + HQ blend (200 ppm total)
  • Filler: Fine CaCO₃ (60 phr)
  • Thickener: ZnO (2.0 phr)
  • IMR: Proprietary ester blend (2.5 phr)
  • LPA: PVAc (10 phr)
  • Pigment: TiO₂ + color (3 phr)
  • Fiber: 20% chopped E-glass (12.5 mm, fine tex)
  • Typical properties: flexural strength 80–110 MPa, shrinkage < 0.1%

17.2.3 High-temperature / under-hood SMC

Target: HDT > 180 °C, low moisture absorption, chemical resistance. Used for battery trays, intake manifolds, electrical enclosures.

  • Resin: DCPD-modified UPR or Vinyl Ester (100 phr)
  • Styrene: 28 phr
  • Initiator: DTBP (2.0 phr) for high-temperature cure
  • Inhibitor: PBQ (100 ppm)
  • Filler: CaCO₃ + alumina trihydrate (70 phr total)
  • Thickener: MgO (3.0 phr)
  • IMR: PTFE-based (3 phr)
  • LPA: PMMA (8 phr)
  • FR additive: ATH (20 phr)
  • Fiber: 30% chopped E-glass (25 mm)
  • Typical properties: HDT 180–200 °C, flexural strength 100–130 MPa

17.2.4 Fire-retardant (FR) SMC

Target: UL 94 V-0 or equivalent, low smoke. Used for public transport, electrical cabinets, aircraft interiors.

  • Resin: Phenolic resol (100 phr)
  • Styrene: 0 phr (phenolic uses no styrene)
  • Initiator: Peroxide or acid catalyst (3 phr)
  • Inhibitor: None or minimal
  • Filler: ATH + Mg(OH)₂ blend (80 phr)
  • Thickener: Specialized for phenolic (2 phr)
  • IMR: Custom release for phenolic (2 phr)
  • Fiber: 25% E-glass or S-2 glass (25 mm)
  • Typical properties: LOI > 40%, flexural strength 70–100 MPa

17.2.5 Structural epoxy SMC

Target: maximum Tg, low shrinkage, high dimensional stability. Used for aerospace secondary structures, EV battery frames, high-performance automotive nodes.

  • Resin: Bisphenol-A epoxy (100 phr)
  • Diluent: Reactive diluent (e.g., butyl glycidyl ether, 10 phr)
  • Hardener: Dicyandiamide or aromatic amine (15 phr)
  • Accelerator: Imidazole (2 phr)
  • Filler: CaCO₃ + silica (50 phr)
  • Thickener: Urea-based (1.5 phr)
  • IMR: Silicone-based (2 phr)
  • Coupling agent: Amino silane (0.3 phr)
  • Fiber: 40% S-2 glass or carbon (25 mm)
  • Typical properties: Tg 180–220 °C, flexural strength 140–200 MPa

17.3 Formulation trade-off matrix

No single formulation is optimal for all properties. Use this matrix to understand the directional effects of key formulation changes.

17.4 Low-styrene and styrene-free formulations

Regulatory pressure is driving the industry toward reduced styrene content. Below 30 wt% styrene, the paste viscosity rises sharply and the cure kinetics change, requiring reformulation across multiple ingredients.

17.4.1 Alternative diluents

  • Vinyl toluene: similar reactivity to styrene; reduces VOC by ~15%
  • Diallyl phthalate (DAP): higher boiling point; lower VOC but slower cure
  • Methacrylate monomers (e.g., isobornyl methacrylate): low odor, high reactivity; used in low-styrene systems
  • Reactive prepolymers: low-viscosity urethane or epoxy diluents; expensive but effective

17.4.2 Reformulation checklist for low-styrene systems

  1. Increase initiator level by 20–50% to compensate for slower diffusion
  2. Adjust thickener type and level to restore maturation behavior
  3. Verify LPA compatibility with new monomer system
  4. Update inhibitor balance to prevent premature inhibition
  5. Re-validate viscosity evolution and gel time at molding temperature

17.5 Formulation troubleshooting cheat sheet

Symptom Likely Ingredient Cause Check First Fix
Paste too thick / hard to chop Filler level, thickener type, LPA Paste viscosity at 25 °C Reduce filler or switch to finer grade; check thickener dispersion
Premature cure / short pot life Initiator, inhibitor, storage temp Initiator concentration; storage logs Increase inhibitor or reduce initiator; cool storage
Poor surface / orange peel LPA level, filler grind, IMR LPA type / loading; filler PSD Increase LPA; use finer filler; verify IMR dispersion
Low impact strength Fiber content, fiber length, thermoplastic Burn-off; chop length; thermoplastic % Increase fiber content or length; add thermoplastic modifier
High moisture absorption Resin type, filler type, fiber sizing Resin family; filler CaCO₃ vs silica Switch to iso-UPR or VE; use hydrophobic filler coating
Excessive smoke / poor FST Resin, FR additive, filler Resin family; ATH/Mg(OH)₂ level Switch to phenolic; increase ATH loading; verify dispersion
Poor fiber wet-out Surfactant, resin viscosity, styrene Paste appearance; fiber pull-out in polished sections Add surfactant; reduce filler; check styrene level
Color shift / pigment floating Pigment type / loading, resin compatibility Paste color uniformity; dispersion microscopy Use predispersed pigment masterbatch; verify compatibility

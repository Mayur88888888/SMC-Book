Resin Systems: Types, Chemistry, and Selection

Why resin selection is the first decision

The resin matrix determines the processing window (viscosity, gel time, cure temperature), the achievable mechanical and thermal properties (Tg, HDT, strength, toughness), cost, and downstream compatibility (paintability, chemical resistance, fire performance). Choosing the wrong resin family cannot be corrected by process optimization alone.

1A.1 Unsaturated Polyester (UPR)

UPR is the dominant resin system in SMC, accounting for >85% of global production volume. UPR consists of an unsaturated polyester backbone dissolved in a reactive diluent (typically styrene at 30–45 wt%), which serves as both a viscosity reducer and a crosslinking co-monomer. Cure occurs via free-radical copolymerization of the polyester double bonds with styrene, initiated by organic peroxides at elevated temperature.

UPR backbone variants

Orthophthalic (Ortho-UPR)

  • Lowest cost; standard for non-demanding applications
  • Moderate water resistance; Tg typically 70–110 °C
  • Good processability; moderate shrinkage (6–10% linear without LPA)
  • Typical uses: general structural, non-cosmetic parts, commodity grades

Isophthalic (Iso-UPR)

  • Better hydrolytic stability and chemical resistance than ortho
  • Higher Tg range (100–140 °C); better mechanical properties
  • ~15–25% cost premium over ortho
  • Typical uses: Class A automotive, chemical-resistant parts, marine

DCPD-modified UPR

  • Dicyclopentadiene modification provides lower styrene demand and lower cost
  • Good surface quality; lower water absorption than ortho
  • Tg typically 80–120 °C; moderate mechanical properties
  • Typical uses: automotive body panels, recreational vehicles

Hybrid / specialty UPR

  • Bisphenol-A fumarate: high chemical resistance; used in corrosion-grade FRP
  • Chlorendic: inherent flame retardancy; niche electrical/FR applications
  • These are rarely used in high-volume SMC but may appear in specialty grades

1A.2 Vinyl Ester (VE)

Vinyl ester resins are formed by reacting an epoxy resin (typically bisphenol-A diglycidyl ether) with methacrylic acid. The resulting backbone has reactive double bonds only at the chain ends (unlike UPR which has internal unsaturation), providing:

  • Superior corrosion resistance: fewer ester linkages exposed to hydrolysis
  • Higher toughness and elongation: 4–7% vs 1–3% for typical UPR
  • Better fatigue resistance: important for structural and cyclic-load applications
  • Tg range: 100–150 °C; HDT up to 170 °C for novolac VE variants
  • Cost: 1.5–2.5× UPR; justified when corrosion, toughness, or fatigue are critical

Novolac vinyl esters (epoxy novolac backbone) push Tg above 150 °C and provide excellent solvent resistance, used in aggressive chemical environments.

1A.3 Epoxy SMC

Epoxy-based SMC is used when maximum mechanical performance, thermal stability, and dimensional precision are required. Epoxy SMC typically uses latent hardener systems (dicyandiamide, aromatic amines, or anhydrides) that provide long shelf life and rapid cure at elevated temperature (140–180 °C).

Advantages

  • Highest Tg achievable (150–220 °C)
  • Excellent adhesion to fibers (lower sizing sensitivity)
  • Low cure shrinkage (1–3%)
  • Superior dimensional stability and creep resistance

Limitations

  • Cost: 3–5× UPR
  • Longer cure times at comparable temperatures
  • No styrene diluent → higher initial viscosity; different thickening chemistry
  • Brittleness unless toughened (rubber or thermoplastic modifiers)

Typical applications: aerospace secondary structures, automotive structural nodes (e.g., B-pillar reinforcements), high-temperature under-hood components, and EV battery structural elements.

1A.4 Phenolic and Specialty Resins

Phenolic (resol) SMC is selected when fire/smoke/toxicity (FST) performance is the primary driver. Phenolic resins offer:

  • LOI > 35–45% without halogenated FR additives
  • Very low smoke generation (critical for rail, mass transit, tunnels, aircraft interiors)
  • HDT > 150 °C; good dimensional stability at elevated temperature
  • Limitations: darker color (brown/black), brittleness, water condensate during cure, more aggressive on tooling

Other specialty systems include BMI (bismaleimide) for aerospace >200 °C applications and cyanate ester for low dielectric loss radome applications. These are very niche and rarely seen in volume SMC production.

1A.5 Low-Profile Additives (LPAs)

LPAs are thermoplastic polymers added to the resin paste (5–15 phr) that compensate for cure shrinkage by phase-separating during cure and creating microvoids that offset polymerization contraction. This is critical for Class A automotive surfaces.

1A.6 Styrene and Reactive Diluents

Styrene serves a dual role: (1) viscosity reducer — makes the paste processable, and (2) crosslinking co-monomer — forms the cured network. Typical styrene content in UPR-based SMC paste is 30–45 wt% of the resin.

Emissions and regulatory pressure

Styrene is classified as a possible carcinogen (IARC Group 2A). Workplace exposure limits (20–50 ppm TWA depending on jurisdiction) and VOC regulations are driving the industry toward low-styrene (<30%) and styrene-free formulations using alternative diluents (e.g., vinyl toluene, diallyl phthalate, methacrylate monomers). Styrene-free UPR typically costs 20–40% more and may require reformulation of thickening and cure systems.

1A.7 Initiator (Catalyst) Systems

Free-radical initiators decompose at elevated temperature to generate radicals that trigger crosslinking. The choice of initiator controls the onset temperature and cure rate profile.

Initiator Abbreviation 10 h t½ (°C) Activation Range (°C) Typical Use
Benzoyl peroxide BPO 73 75–95 Low-temperature kick-start; dual-initiator "low" component
tert-Butyl peroxybenzoate TBPB 104 100–130 Standard SMC initiator; most common single-initiator choice
Di-tert-butyl peroxide DTBP 124 125–155 High-temperature "finishing" initiator; ensures high final cure
tert-Butyl peroxy-2-ethylhexanoate TBPEH 74 70–90 Fast low-temperature cure; short cycle times

Dual-initiator strategy for thick parts

For parts >10 mm thick, a dual-initiator system (e.g., TBPEH + TBPB or BPO + DTBP) provides staged cure: the low-temperature initiator starts cure uniformly before the exotherm develops, while the high-temperature initiator ensures complete cure in the interior where exotherm peaks can exceed the mold temperature by 20–60 °C (see Ch06).

1A.8 Inhibitors and Shelf Life

Inhibitors (radical scavengers) are added at low concentrations (50–500 ppm) to prevent premature polymerization during storage and paste mixing. Common inhibitors:

  • BHT (butylated hydroxytoluene): general-purpose; effective at 100–300 ppm
  • Hydroquinone (HQ): strong inhibition; can cause yellowing; 50–200 ppm
  • p-Benzoquinone (PBQ): fast-acting; used in combination systems
  • MEHQ (monomethyl ether of hydroquinone): standard styrene stabilizer; 15–50 ppm in monomer

Shelf life of uncured SMC is typically 2–6 months at 18–25 °C, depending on resin reactivity, inhibitor level, and thickener type. Higher storage temperatures accelerate inhibitor consumption and maturation advance, shortening the useful molding window.

1A.9 Resin Selection Guide

How to use this table

Start with the application requirements in the left column, then read across for the recommended resin family and the key tradeoffs. This is a starting-point guide — verify with supplier datasheets and lab trials for your specific formulation.

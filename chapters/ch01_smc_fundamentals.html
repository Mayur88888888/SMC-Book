SMC fundamentals: constituents, maturation, and incoming QC

What makes SMC different from “generic press molding compound”

SMC (sheet molding compound) is a thermoset composite molding feedstock designed to: (i) be handled as sheet charges, (ii) flow under compression to fill complex molds, (iii) cure rapidly under heat and pressure to produce net-shape parts. Unlike continuous-fiber laminates, SMC is dominated by chopped reinforcement architecture and a strongly evolving viscosity as cure advances.

Constituent roles (engineering view)

Resin system

  • Provides flow before gelation and forms the crosslinked network.
  • Thermal history controls the state of cure, which in turn controls properties such as Tg, viscosity, cure shrinkage, stiffness, and thermal expansion.

Reinforcement (chopped fiber)

  • Primary load-carrying phase for structural SMC grades.
  • Strongly influences flow: fiber network effects can shift behavior away from simple resin squeeze flow.

Fillers / additives

  • Control cost, shrinkage, surface quality, flame retardancy, and processing viscosity.
  • Change thermal conductivity and heat capacity → affects heat-up and cure uniformity.

Internal mold release & surface packages

  • Enable demolding; also interact with paintability and surface defect formation.
  • Demolding/tool cleaning can be a damage source if not controlled (surface scratches/gouges; possible integrity issues).

Maturation / storage (why “same recipe” can behave differently)

Many thermoset SMC systems are designed to change rheological state during storage (“maturation”). Practically, this means the same nominal formulation can show different flow in the press depending on: storage temperature, storage time, and out-time before molding.

Key linkage to the rest of the book

Because viscosity depends on temperature and state of cure, and viscosity governs flow and void suppression, your incoming material condition (including maturation state) couples directly to porosity and surface finish outcomes.

Incoming material QC checklist (minimum set)

  • Sheet thickness and areal weight uniformity (map across roll)
  • Fiber content (burn-off or other validated method)
  • Volatiles/moisture (especially for cosmetic and electrical/FR grades)
  • Simple flow surrogate test at molding temperature (production-friendly QC)
  • Visual inspection: dry spots, resin-rich streaks, contamination

What “quality” means by application

  • Cosmetic panels: surface waviness, print-through, pinholes/porosity, paint defects.
  • Structural: porosity level, knit-line strength, fiber orientation distribution, dimensional repeatability.
  • Electrical / FR: flame performance, tracking/creep resistance, low voids (dielectric reliability), dimensional stability.

Compaction, consolidation, and thickness control

Connection to quality

Cure cycle variations can change resin flow prior to cure, level of cure, and the finished thickness. Thickness variation can affect consolidation and curing uniformity. [1]

7.1 Definitions: compaction vs consolidation (useful distinction)

  • Compaction: reduction of bulk thickness under applied pressure as the charge spreads and densifies.
  • Consolidation: elimination/reduction of internal voids and achievement of intimate contact within the material and against the mold surface.

In thermoset SMC, consolidation is time-dependent because viscosity increases during cure (Ch04). This means consolidation capability is high early and decreases near gelation.

7.2 Press pressure/force and thickness: what you can control

The press controls force (or pressure) and displacement (closing position). The part thickness outcome is governed by:

  • Tooling geometry and stop conditions (fixed cavity height vs force-controlled shutoff)
  • SMC bulk compressibility and flow resistance (depends on temperature and cure state)
  • Charge mass distribution (local mass surplus drives local flow and local pressure gradients)
  • Press/mold compliance (platen deflection, tool bending, thermal expansion)

7.3 Why thickness varies (root causes)

7.3.1 Press and tool mechanics

  • Platen parallelism errors: creates systematic thickness wedge.
  • Platen/tool deflection: center vs edge thickness differences depending on load path and stiffness.
  • Thermal expansion gradients: uneven heating can change tool dimensions and local cavity gap.

7.3.2 Material and cycle-induced causes

  • Charge mass variability: thickness tends to increase where mass is high if flow is restricted.
  • Charge maturity variability: shifts viscosity window; can lead to earlier “freeze” and incomplete spread.
  • Cure cycle variability: changes flow prior to cure and can change finished thickness. [1]

7.4 Thickness variation → nonuniform cure and consolidation (mechanism)

The handbook excerpt directly notes that thickness variation affects consolidation and curing uniformity. [1] Here is the mechanism chain you should use in troubleshooting:

  1. Thickness varies → local heat-up rate changes (Ch06).
  2. Temperature history varies → local cure rate varies (Ch05).
  3. Cure varies → viscosity rise timing varies (Ch04).
  4. Viscosity and pressure effectiveness vary → local void content and surface replication vary (Ch08, Ch12).

Practical diagnostic: thickness map + defect map

  • Make a thickness heatmap (grid measurement).
  • Overlay porosity indicators (pinholes, density, micro-CT if available).
  • If defects track thickness gradients, treat it as a coupled thermal/compaction problem, not only “venting.”

7.6 Press curve signatures (how compaction appears in data)

  • Early stage: force rises gradually as thickness decreases; material flows.
  • Approach to cavity fill: force slope increases (less free volume, more resistance to flow).
  • Near gelation: force can spike for a given displacement because material stiffens and relaxes more slowly.

In Ch11, we formalize this as “golden curves” for process monitoring.

7.7 Control plan: how to make thickness robust

  1. Tool and press qualification: verify platen parallelism under load; verify tool stiffness and shutoff behavior.
  2. Thermal qualification: verify temperature uniformity across tool surface (Ch06).
  3. Charge control: control mass, dimensions, and placement tolerances (Ch03).
  4. Cycle control: control closing speed and dwell to stabilize flow before viscosity rise (Ch10).
  5. Routine measurement: thickness mapping on sampling plan; correlate with press curve drift.

Defects atlas: symptom → mechanism → check → fix

Handbook anchors for troubleshooting logic

(i) Cure-cycle variations (time/temperature/pressure) can change resin flow prior to cure, degree of cure, and finished thickness; thickness variation affects consolidation and curing uniformity. [1] (ii) Processing/cure parameters (time–temperature, vacuum/pressure, viscosity/flow) influence porosity, Tg, and surface finish. [3] (iii) Demolding and tool cleaning can cause laminate integrity issues and surface scratches/gouges. [3]

13.1 How to troubleshoot SMC defects (method, not guessing)

  1. Classify the defect: fill defect, void/porosity, surface/cosmetic, dimensional/warpage, demold damage.
  2. Localize it: where in the part? near end-of-fill, near knit lines, near thickness transitions, near vents?
  3. Check the cycle record: temperature/pressure/time compliance and press curve signature (Ch11).
  4. Check tooling condition: vents, parting line, flash land, release state (Ch09).
  5. Check material condition: storage/out-time/maturity, moisture/volatiles (Ch01).
  6. Change one variable at a time in structured trials or DOE (Ch16).

13.3 Short shot / incomplete fill

Likely mechanisms

  • viscosity rises too early (tool too hot, material too reactive/mature)
  • insufficient time in low-viscosity window due to close profile
  • insufficient pressure/tonnage for feature filling
  • poor charge placement: excessive flow distance (Ch03)

What to check

  • press force-position curve: early spike suggesting premature sealing or high resistance
  • tool temperature uniformity and setpoint drift
  • charge coverage ratio and placement repeatability

Corrective actions

  • increase coverage ratio / redesign charge pattern to reduce flow distance
  • adjust close profile: longer venting stage, different packing timing
  • reduce cure acceleration early (temperature profile), if feasible
  • verify tool vents: blocked vents can create back-pressure and stop flow

13.4 Flash / excessive resin bleed

Likely mechanisms

  • excess charge mass or poor mass distribution
  • flash land/shutoff design or wear (Ch09)
  • pressure too high too early while viscosity is low

What to check

  • part mass trend and flash mass trend (material loss)
  • tool condition: lands, shutoffs, alignment
  • press curve: unusually low resistance early (too fluid) plus high pressure

Corrective actions

  • tighten charge mass tolerance and placement tolerance
  • modify close/pressure schedule: delay full packing pressure until after major flow/venting
  • inspect and rework flash lands/shutoffs if worn

13.5 Porosity / pinholes / internal voids

Likely mechanisms

  • air entrapment due to charge placement sealing pockets (Ch03)
  • blocked/insufficient venting (Ch08–Ch09)
  • insufficient pressure or wrong pressure timing for void collapse
  • volatiles/moisture outgassing during heat-up

The handbook excerpt explicitly links porosity level to time–temperature profile, vacuum/pressure, viscosity/flow. [3]

What to check

  • vent condition and vent cleaning logs
  • close profile: did vents seal too early?
  • tool temperature uniformity; thick sections and exotherm risk (Ch06) [2]
  • incoming material: storage/out-time, moisture indicators

Corrective actions

  • clean/restore vents; add vents at end-of-fill and knit-line regions
  • modify close profile to increase effective venting time before packing
  • adjust vacuum/pressure scheduling where possible
  • stabilize material conditioning and handling (temperature, out-time)

13.6 Blisters (often after painting or post-heat exposure)

Likely mechanisms

  • subsurface voids expand during post-heat
  • volatile/moisture-driven gas generation not fully removed during molding
  • insufficient cure state (low Tg) for paint bake temperature window

What to check

  • Tg/cure state measurements vs paint bake temperature
  • porosity mapping (cross-sections, CT if available)
  • thermal history: thick-part exotherm peaks (Ch06) [2]

Corrective actions

  • tighten porosity controls (Ch08) and venting/close profile controls (Ch10)
  • ensure cure completion targets are met; adjust dwell as needed
  • improve incoming moisture control and handling (Ch01)

13.7 Knit line visibility and knit-line weakness

Likely mechanisms

  • flow fronts meet late when viscosity is high (limited healing)
  • air trapped at the meeting interface
  • charge pattern creates unavoidable front meetings in critical zones (Ch03)

Cure-cycle variability changes resin flow prior to cure and can alter knit-line formation and quality. [1]

What to check

  • short-shot studies to visualize flow and meeting lines
  • press curve and temperature history: early viscosity rise?
  • vent locations near predicted knit lines

Corrective actions

  • move knit lines by changing charge pattern/coverage ratio
  • improve venting and closing profile around meeting fronts
  • adjust temperature profile to preserve low-viscosity time for healing (within cure constraints)

13.8 Burn / thermal degradation marks

Likely mechanisms

  • local overheating due to tool hot spots
  • thick-part exotherm peak leading to high interior temperature [2]
  • excess dwell at high temperature

What to check

  • tool temperature map; look for zone imbalance
  • embedded thermocouple data during trials (Ch06)
  • color/odor changes localized to thick regions or near heaters

Corrective actions

  • reduce hot spots; improve thermal uniformity
  • modify cycle to control exotherm (temperature ramp/dwell changes)
  • validate kinetics/thermal model for thick parts (Ch16)

13.9 Warpage / distortion / twist

Likely mechanisms

  • nonuniform cure and thermal gradients (Ch06)
  • thickness variation changing consolidation/cure uniformity [1]
  • tool constraint and friction during cure/cooldown (Ch14)

What to check

  • thickness map vs deformation map correlation
  • tool temperature nonuniformity
  • demold temperature (too early demold can relax into distortion)

Corrective actions

  • improve thickness control (Ch07) and thermal uniformity (Ch06)
  • adjust cure completion before demold; consider controlled cooling where feasible
  • review geometry for asymmetric stiffness and thickness transitions

13.10 Surface scratches, gouges, demold damage

Likely mechanisms

  • tool surface contamination or damage
  • insufficient release or incorrect demold timing
  • aggressive tool cleaning practices

Demolding and tool cleaning can cause scratches/gouges and may affect laminate integrity. [3]

What to check

  • tool surface inspection under proper lighting
  • ejection system alignment and pin witness distribution
  • cleaning chemicals and procedures

Corrective actions

  • standardize cleaning; avoid abrasive methods that change tool surface
  • restore tool surface finish; repair damaged lands/vent edges
  • optimize demold criteria (cure state + temperature) and release management

Testing and qualification: linking process to performance

Why qualification belongs in a process book

Without a structured test plan, you cannot distinguish “good part” from “lucky part.” Qualification links process windows (temperature, pressure, time, charge design) to measurable outcomes: porosity, cure state, thickness uniformity, mechanical properties, and surface quality.

15.1 The qualification hierarchy

SMC parts span widely different end-use requirements. A single test program cannot serve all. Use this hierarchy to plan sampling and acceptance:

Level 1 — Process conformance

  • Press curve compliance (Ch11)
  • Thickness mapping
  • Mass / density uniformity
  • Surface class / porosity rating

Level 2 — Material / microstructure

  • Degree of cure (DSC, DMA Tg)
  • Void content (density, microscopy, CT)
  • Fiber orientation / distribution
  • Filler dispersion

Level 3 — Mechanical / functional

  • Tensile, flexural, compressive strength
  • Interlaminar shear (ILSS)
  • Impact (Charpy / falling weight)
  • Fatigue and creep (structural grades)

Level 4 — Environmental / end-use

  • HDT / continuous service temperature
  • Chemical resistance / hydrolysis
  • Flame / smoke / toxicity (FST)
  • Paint adhesion and weathering

15.2 Process conformance tests (fast, production-friendly)

These tests should be performed on every production run or on a statistically valid sampling plan. They link directly to the control logic in Ch11.

15.2.1 Press signature monitoring

Record and evaluate force-position-time traces against golden curves. Key metrics:

  • Peak force and position at peak
  • Slope of packing region (indicates flow resistance)
  • Time to reach target force/dwell transition
  • Any early force spikes (venting / sealing anomalies)

15.2.2 Dimensional checks

  • Thickness map: grid measurement (e.g., 5×5 or 10×10 points) to detect wedging or local thin/thick zones
  • Flatness / warpage: CMM or 3D scan vs nominal CAD (Ch14)
  • Critical feature dimensions: hole diameters, rib widths, boss heights

15.2.3 Mass and density

Part mass is a rapid proxy for charge mass consistency, flash control, and compaction. Density = mass / measured volume. Compare to theoretical density based on formulation: 

rho_theoretical = 1 / ( wf/rho_f + wr/rho_r + wc/rho_c )

where w = weight fraction and ρ = component density.

15.3 Microstructure and cure-state tests

15.3.1 Degree of cure (DSC)

DSC measures residual heat of reaction. A fully cured sample shows no exotherm on a second heat. Calculate: 

DOC = 1 - (residual exotherm) / (total exotherm from fully uncured resin)

Typical acceptance: DOC ≥ 0.90–0.95 for structural parts; ≥ 0.85 for non-critical cosmetic parts.

15.3.2 Glass transition temperature (DMA / DSC)

Tg is a practical proxy for cure state in production. DMA (storage modulus peak or tan δ peak) is more sensitive than DSC for highly crosslinked systems.

  • DSC Tg: midpoint of heat-capacity step
  • DMA Tg: peak of tan δ (Tgα) or onset of storage modulus drop (Tge)
  • For many SMC grades, Tg should exceed maximum service temperature + 20–40 °C margin

15.3.3 Void content measurement

Acid digestion / burn-off

  • Dissolve resin in acid; weigh residual fiber
  • Fast and accurate for fiber content
  • Can estimate void content if density known

Micro-CT scanning

  • 3D void mapping without sectioning
  • Detects sub-surface pinholes, knit-line voids
  • Best for root-cause analysis; costly for routine

15.4 Mechanical property tests

Select mechanical tests based on the failure modes expected in service. Always report test direction relative to flow/fiber orientation.

15.4.1 Tensile (ASTM D638 / ISO 527)

  • Sensitive to fiber orientation and void content
  • Test both “flow direction” and “transverse” to capture anisotropy
  • Typical range: 40–250 MPa depending on fiber loading and resin

15.4.2 Flexural (ASTM D790 / ISO 178)

  • Most common for SMC panel qualification
  • Reflects both matrix and fiber contribution
  • Typical flexural strength: 80–250 MPa; modulus: 8–25 GPa

15.4.3 Compression (ASTM D695 / ISO 604)

  • Critical for structural nodes (B-pillars, front-end modules)
  • Sensitive to fiber buckling and void location
  • Test both flat-wise and edge-wise

15.4.4 Impact

  • Charpy / Izod (ASTM D256 / ISO 180): notched and unnotched; measures toughness and crack resistance
  • Falling weight (Dart / Gardner impact): simulates real impact events; gives energy-to-failure
  • For automotive, falling-weight tests on full panels or sub-structures are often required

15.5 Environmental and durability tests

Thermal

  • HDT per ASTM D648
  • Continuous thermal aging (1000 h at elevated T)
  • Thermal cycling (thermal shock)

Chemical / moisture

  • Water absorption (24 h, 7 days, saturation)
  • Immersion in fuel, brake fluid, coolant
  • Salt spray (ASTM B117) for corrosion-prone assemblies

Fire / smoke / toxicity

  • UL 94 (horizontal / vertical burn)
  • LOI (ASTM D2863)
  • NBS smoke chamber (ASTM E662)
  • FAR 25.853 (aircraft cabin materials)

Weathering / UV

  • QUV / accelerated weathering (ASTM G154)
  • Florida exposure (natural weathering)
  • Paint adhesion after weathering (cross-cut + tape test)

15.6 Application-specific qualification plans

15.6.1 Automotive Class A body panel

  • Process: thickness uniformity, mass, surface gloss/DOI, pinhole count
  • Microstructure: void content < 1–2%, Tg > 110 °C
  • Mechanical: flexural strength ≥ 100 MPa, impact energy-to-failure per OEM spec
  • Environmental: paint adhesion, thermal cycling (–40 to +80 °C), humidity resistance
  • Aging: 6-month outdoor exposure or equivalent QUV; no blistering or fiber print-through

15.6.2 Structural truck / heavy-duty

  • Process: thickness map, mass, porosity rating (ultrasonic or density)
  • Microstructure: void content < 2%, fiber content ± 2 wt%, Tg > 120 °C
  • Mechanical: tensile, flexural, compressive, ILSS; impact ≥ 800 J/m notched
  • Environmental: long-term creep at 60 °C, chemical resistance to road fluids

15.6.3 Aerospace interior / secondary structure

  • Process: tight thickness control, void mapping (micro-CT preferred)
  • Microstructure: Tg > 150 °C (epoxy SMC), FST compliance verified
  • Mechanical: compression after impact (CAI), open-hole compression, edge delamination resistance
  • Environmental: thermal cycling to –55 °C, humidity soak, FST per FAR 25.853

15.7 Sampling and statistics

Qualification is meaningless without a sampling plan. Use the following framework:

  1. Define the population: by lot, shift, mold, resin batch, or fiber batch
  2. Choose sampling frequency: every part (destructive tests) vs periodic (non-destructive)
  3. Use control charts: X-bar and R charts for thickness, mass, Tg, flexural strength
  4. Set action limits: not just specification limits—define when a trend, not just a point, requires investigation
  5. Link to golden curves: process conformance metrics should correlate with mechanical outcome metrics

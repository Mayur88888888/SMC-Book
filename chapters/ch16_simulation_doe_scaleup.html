Simulation, DOE, scaling, and robustness

Why simulation and DOE matter for SMC

SMC processing involves coupled physics: heat transfer, chemical reaction, flow, and solid mechanics. Experiments alone cannot cover all combinations of thickness, charge pattern, resin system, and tool design. Simulation + DOE provide a structured path from material data to production recipes.

16.1 The coupled physics of SMC compression molding

A complete simulation must resolve interactions among:

  1. Thermochemistry: energy balance with reaction heat, temperature-dependent kinetics
  2. Rheology: viscosity depends on temperature and degree of cure (Ch04–Ch05)
  3. Flow/compaction: squeeze flow, feature filling, thickness reduction, fiber orientation evolution
  4. Solid mechanics: shrinkage, thermal stress, warpage under tool constraint (Ch14)

The excerpted literature context emphasizes multi-submodel coupling in curing simulations. [10] In practice, you may start with reduced-order models and add complexity as needed.

16.2 Minimal modeling set for engineering decisions

If you cannot build a full multiphysics model immediately, the minimum set that improves robustness is:

Thermochemical model

  • 1D conduction + reaction heat generation
  • Predicts interior temperature and DOC history
  • Input: cp, k, Hr, kinetic parameters (Ch05–Ch06)

Viscosity model

  • Castro–Macosko or equivalent cure-dependent form
  • Predicts flow window closure
  • Input: Arrhenius parameters, gel point (Ch04)

Compaction model

  • Empirical or physics-based thickness vs pressure relationship
  • Predicts final part thickness and fiber volume fraction
  • Input: press force, tool geometry (Ch07)

Stress/warpage model

  • Cure shrinkage + thermal contraction under constraint
  • Predicts residual stress and part distortion
  • Input: anisotropic CTE, modulus evolution (Ch14)

16.3 Design of Experiments (DOE) for SMC process development

DOE is the most reliable way to separate the effects of temperature, pressure, time, charge design, and material condition. Use a structured sequence:

16.3.1 Phase 1: Screening

Identify which factors actually matter for your defect mode. Use a fractional factorial (2^(k-p)) or Plackett-Burman design. Typical screening factors:

  • Tool temperature (high / low)
  • Closing speed (fast / slow)
  • Packing pressure (high / low)
  • Charge mass (high / low)
  • Charge placement (pattern A / pattern B)
  • Material out-time (fresh / aged)

16.3.2 Phase 2: Optimization

For the significant factors from Phase 1, use a response-surface design (e.g., Central Composite) to map the process window and find optimal settings for:

  • Minimum porosity
  • Target flexural strength
  • Minimum thickness variation
  • Maximum surface quality score

16.3.3 Phase 3: Robustness

Verify that the optimal recipe is insensitive to normal manufacturing variability. Deliberately vary noise factors (material maturity, tool temperature drift, charge mass scatter) and confirm that the response remains within specification.

16.4 Scale-up: from pilot to production

Scaling SMC from prototype to production is not simply “make it bigger.” Key translation rules:

Thickness scaling

  • Heat-up time increases with the square of thickness
  • Exotherm risk grows nonlinearly in thick sections
  • Flow distance may need reduction or multiple charges
  • Clamp tonnage must scale with projected area, not just thickness

Tooling scaling

  • Temperature uniformity harder to maintain across large platens
  • Vent spacing must increase; vent count grows with cavity size
  • Tool stiffness must prevent deflection under full tonnage
  • Ejection system must handle larger areas without part distortion

16.4.1 The “similarity criteria” checklist

When scaling, maintain these dimensionless or ratio-based invariants where possible:

  • Coverage ratio: keep constant to preserve flow distance scaling
  • Biot number for heat transfer: Bi = hL/k; if Bi changes, thermal response changes
  • Filling time / gel time ratio: should remain < 0.8 for robust fill
  • Pressure / viscosity ratio: governs compaction and void collapse (Ch08)

16.5 Model calibration and validation workflow

  1. Material characterization: DSC kinetics, rheology, thermal properties (cp, k), cure shrinkage
  2. Model parameterization: fit kinetic model, viscosity model, compaction law
  3. Validation on benchmark geometry: predict and compare against instrumented trials (temperature traces, press curves, final DOC)
  4. Iterative refinement: adjust parameters until predictions match within acceptable tolerance
  5. Production application: use model for recipe generation, window mapping, and risk assessment

Practical rule

Never trust a model more than one geometry away from validation data. If you validate on a 2 mm flat plaque, do not immediately trust predictions for a 12 mm ribbed structural part without additional validation.

16.6 Digital twin and advanced control preview

In advanced manufacturing, a digital twin couples real-time sensor data (Ch11) with a running simulation to predict part state during the cycle. Benefits:

  • Predict DOC and temperature before demold without destructive tests
  • Detect deviations from expected behavior early (e.g., vent blockage, material drift)
  • Enable adaptive control: adjust pressure or dwell in real time to hit targets

Digital twins require robust sensor integration, fast solvers, and validated models. They are an evolution of the golden curve method (Ch11), not a replacement for good process fundamentals.

16.7 Industrialization checklist

  1. Material data package: kinetics, rheology, thermal properties, shrinkage data
  2. Baseline process recipe: temperature, pressure, time, charge design locked
  3. Instrumented trials: temperature traces, press curves, dimensional checks
  4. DOE-driven optimization: screen, optimize, verify robustness
  5. Qualification plan: Level 1–4 tests per Ch15, with sampling plan
  6. Control plan: golden curves, reaction plans, SPC limits (Ch11)
  7. Documentation: traveler fields, recipe management, deviation tracking

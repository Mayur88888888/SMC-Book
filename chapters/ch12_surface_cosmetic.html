Surface and cosmetic quality: paintability, print-through, demolding

Handbook-anchored linkage

Processing parameters (time–temperature profile, vacuum/pressure, flow/viscosity) influence surface finish quality and Tg. [3] Demolding and tool cleaning can damage laminate integrity and surface finish (scratches/gouges). [3]

12.1 What “surface quality” means in SMC (different end uses)

  • Class-A automotive panel: waviness, gloss, pinholes, fiber print-through, flow marks; paint-readiness.
  • Structural part: surface may be secondary, but surface defects can signal porosity and consolidation issues.
  • Electrical/FR enclosure: surface can affect sealing, tracking resistance, and contamination retention.

12.2 Surface formation mechanisms (process-physics view)

12.2.1 Resin-rich “skin” and replication of mold texture

During early flow and packing, the material at the tool surface experiences high shear, temperature, and pressure. A resin-rich surface layer can form depending on formulation and flow behavior. Surface replication is better when:

  • surface-side viscosity is sufficiently low early in the cycle (heat-up + low cure)
  • pressure is adequate and maintained while the surface is still able to flow/relax
  • tool temperature uniformity is good (Ch06)

12.2.2 Flow marks, “witness lines,” and knit-line visibility

  • Flow fronts meeting can create subtle surface lines, especially if trapped gas or resin-rich streaks exist (Ch03, Ch08).
  • Variability in viscosity/flow state across the cavity can leave nonuniform surface texture. [3]

12.2.3 Pinhole mechanisms

Pinholes are frequently a surface expression of internal porosity:

  • entrapped air not vented (Ch08)
  • volatile/moisture outgassing during heat-up
  • gas expansion amplified by temperature rise/exotherm in thick regions (Ch06)

12.3 Paintability: why coatings amplify small defects

Coatings can amplify:

  • pinhole visibility (porosity becomes a crater)
  • waviness (gloss highlights long-wavelength surface deviations)
  • flow marks and knit lines (optical contrast)

Because processing parameters influence Tg and surface finish via thermal history and viscosity/flow [3], consistent control of these parameters is often a prerequisite for stable paint quality.

12.4 Process parameters with strongest cosmetic leverage (practical hierarchy)

  1. Venting effectiveness + close profile (air removal; pinholes/porosity) [3]
  2. Tool surface condition and release management (replication, gloss, demold marks)
  3. Tool temperature uniformity (texture consistency; cure state uniformity) [1]
  4. Charge placement consistency (flow uniformity; meeting lines) (Ch03)
  5. Pressure schedule (packing, replication, void collapse) (Ch08–Ch10)

12.5 Demolding and tool cleaning: surface and integrity risks

The handbook excerpt explicitly notes that demolding and tool cleaning can affect laminate integrity and can produce surface scratches and gouges. [3] This is both a cosmetic and structural concern.

12.5.1 Demolding mechanics (what causes damage)

  • High demold force due to insufficient release, inadequate draft, or early demold (part too green).
  • Nonuniform ejection causing bending of a hot/partially cured part (can initiate microcracks or delamination-like damage).
  • Tool surface contamination increasing adhesion and causing tearing or surface transfer.

12.5.2 Tool cleaning discipline (why it must be standardized)

  • Abrasive cleaning can change tool surface roughness and directly create surface defects on parts.
  • Localized scraping can create tool damage that prints onto every subsequent part.
  • Cleaning chemicals can interact with release systems and create inconsistent demolding behavior.
Production rule

Treat demolding and cleaning as controlled process steps with their own work instructions, not as informal operator behavior. If surface damage is observed, investigate tooling condition and cleaning method immediately. [3]

12.6 Inspection and metrics for surface quality

  • Visual standards: defect catalog with severity ranking (pinholes, flow marks, pits, fiber show).
  • Gloss and DOI (distinctness-of-image) for painted cosmetic parts.
  • Surface waviness measurement (profilometry or optical scanning).
  • Correlation with process traces: link surface defects to venting stage and press curve anomalies (Ch11).
